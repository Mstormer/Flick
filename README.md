# Flick

A fast 4K video player for macOS 14 or later (Apple silicon), built to out-seek Leah, IINA and mpv.

- `./make-app.sh` → `build/Flick.app`
- Tests: `Bench/make-formats.sh` makes clips in the FFmpeg-only formats plus damaged ones; `Bench/opentest/opentest file…` opens each off screen (muted, never on the screen the mouse is on) and checks that the clock and picture move, before and after a seek; `Bench/colortest/colortest Media/color/*` measures colour against mpv.
- Keys: ←/→ 5 s (Shift 1 s, Option 30 s), `,`/`.` frame step, space play/pause, `[`/`]` speed steps, ⌫ 1×, 1–4 set speed, ↑/↓ volume (past 100% they raise the Mac's own volume), m mute, f full screen.
- Captions: embedded MP4/MOV tracks (AVPlayer), Matroska text tracks (SRT/ASS/WebVTT), and .srt/.vtt/.ass files next to the video or added with File › Add Subtitle File… (⇧⌘O). View › Subtitles picks the track. Styled by System Settings › Accessibility › Captions; on automatically when that setting prefers captions.
- Control bar: hover over, click or drag the seek bar for a preview picture and time above it (nearest keyframe); speaker button mutes/unmutes and the volume slider sits beside it on the right.
- Playlists: open or drop several files or a folder (recursive, Finder name order) and they play in one window, listed on the left with their lengths; `\` (or L) shows/hides the list. While it shows, ↑/↓ go to the previous/next video and ⌥↑/⌥↓ set the volume; while it's hidden, ↑/↓ are volume and ⌥↑/⌥↓ switch videos. While playing, the list and the info panel fade out with the controls and come back with them; while paused they stay up. Click a row to play it; the next video starts when one ends. While paused, moving through the list doesn't open anything: the picture goes black with the file's name, and the video opens when you press Play. Volume, mute and the info panel carry over.
- File info (I, top right): container, size, duration, overall bitrate and decoder; per track codec with profile/level (H.264, HEVC, VP9, AV1, VC-1, WMV, ProRes…), resolution, frame rate, bit depth, HDR, bitrate; audio codec and profile (AAC-LC, HE-AAC…), channel layout, sample rate, bit depth (lossless), bitrate, language, decoder and which track is playing; subtitle formats.
- Resume: every video reopens where it was left (kept per file, forgotten once watched to the end); Playback › Clear Playback Cache forgets them all.
- Pinch in / spread out on the trackpad resizes the window around its centre, from 320 pt up to the full screen width, keeping the video's shape.
- Menus follow the HIG: every command is in the menu bar (Playback menu lists all seek/frame/speed/volume keys plus your Settings shortcuts); File › Open Recent (with Clear Menu), ⌥R opens the most recent video; Window and Help menus; Help › Flick Help lists every menu shortcut and the trackpad gestures (F full screen, L playlist and Esc leave full screen are keys only).
- Settings (⌘,): configurable shortcut table (action, key, value). Defaults: V show/hide controller, A −0.1×, S +0.2×, Z −10 s, X +10 s, D reset speed (toggles 1× and back), R preferred speed 2× (toggles). Add/remove rows; saved in UserDefaults.
- Opens nearly any video or sound file: MP4/MOV/M4V/3GP natively; AVI (DivX/Xvid, DV, MJPEG, Cinepak, H.264…), VOB/MPG, TS/M2TS, FLV, RealMedia, Ogg/OGV, MXF, NUT, WTV, WMV/ASF and more through the bundled FFmpeg; sound-only files (Opus, Ogg Vorbis, FLAC, APE, WavPack, DTS, AC-3, MP3, AAC…) play with a waveform in place of the picture.
- Damaged files: a file cut short plays up to where its data stops (the timeline ends there too); broken stretches are skipped; when the hardware decoder rejects corrupt frames, playback carries on from the same moment in FFmpeg, which conceals the damage; files nothing can read say so at once.
- Colour: SDR pictures are tagged so macOS shows the encoded values on the display's curve, as IINA/mpv and VLC do, instead of its lighter scene-referred conversion (measured with a grey ramp by `Bench/colortest`: within 1 level of mpv). AVPlayer's own path (HDR, rotated or multi-track MP4/MOV) keeps macOS's rendering.
- MP4/MOV/M4V natively, or via AVPlayer for HDR, rotation, several audio tracks or embedded captions; MKV/WebM read natively (built-in EBML parser + sample-buffer renderers) with H.264, HEVC, AV1 and VP9 video (VP9 through VideoToolbox's hardware decoder). MPEG-1/2 and VC-1 video in MKV play through the bundled FFmpeg, which reads the file as it plays when it decodes every track (fast on network shares; other MKVs are indexed up front). Audio the system can't decode (DTS, TrueHD, Vorbis, ALAC in Matroska) is decoded by the bundled FFmpeg. Apart from the AVPlayer path, sound plays through AVAudioEngine with a time-pitch unit, so speed changes are seamless (no pause or mute; pitch kept); the picture clock follows the audio being heard.
- WMV/ASF (WMV1/2/3, VC-1, MS-MPEG4; WMA, WMA Pro, WMA Lossless) and VP9 the hardware refuses (4:4:4) play through the bundled FFmpeg libraries, decoded in software: frame-exact seeking, frame stepping, seek-bar previews, interlaced VC-1 blended. No remuxing, nothing written to disk.
- FFmpeg: libavformat/libavcodec/libavutil/libswscale/libswresample 9.0.2, LGPL 2.1+, limited to the demuxers and decoders above by `Tools/build-ffmpeg.sh` (run automatically by `make-app.sh` on first build). Shipped as separate dylibs in Contents/Frameworks; notice in Help › Open-Source Licenses.
